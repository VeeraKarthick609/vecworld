# vecworld: Dream Walk design

Date: 2026-10-18 · Status: draft for review · Replaces the meaning-space direction (builds 1–2) as the main game

## 1. Intent

You type a theme ("haunted forest"), wake up standing inside a painted 360° dream of it, and wander: look around freely, walk anywhere you're facing, and discover things as you go. It's a dream, so places don't have to stay the same. Only the step you just took has to feel connected.

**Why the change:** after playing builds 1–2, the user wanted Minecraft-style WASD movement and found the meaning-space scenes "random". They chose to drop the goal of *honestly showing the LLM's meaning-space*. Two goals remain:
1. **A smooth, connected dream world you can walk around in.**
2. **Serious engineering on modest hardware.** Everything runs on one laptop with a 4 GB GPU and serves about 5 friends.

## 2. Goals

- Type a theme and land in a seamless 360° painted panorama of it within about 5 s.
- **Look around instantly and smoothly** (mouse/touch drag, A/D, ←/→) at full browser frame rate, with no generation involved.
- **Walk** (W/↑) in whichever direction you're facing. The next spot is dreamed from the view ahead, so it feels connected, and something new can appear ahead of you.
- **Walk back** (S/↓): turn around and walk. Since it's a dream, where you came from may have changed.
- **Discovery labels:** things that appear ahead (a lantern, a chapel) get a small clickable label on the actual object. Clicking it turns you to face it and walks toward it.
- **Text bar:** typing a new theme mid-walk makes the dream drift toward it over the next few steps instead of cutting.
- **Mood chips** beside the text bar (e.g. `foggy`, `night`, `rain`, `snow`, `eerie`, `warm`): one click nudges the dream's mood. Several can be active at once, and clicking again removes one.
- About 5 people dreaming at once, each in their own dream.

## 3. Non-goals

- A persistent or shared world, or any map. Places are not stored for revisiting.
- Showing the LLM's meaning-space. The vocabulary, mind-map and neighbour hotspots from builds 1–2 are retired.
- Continuous free movement between spots. Walking is step by step, and only *looking* is continuous.
- Scores, quests, accounts, multiplayer interaction.
- Hosted or paid model APIs.

## 4. Anti-goal

**A step that doesn't feel connected to where you were standing.** Close behind it: **a step that feels like nothing changed** (spike round 1: trees just got bigger). Each step should feel like moving *and* reveal something.

## 5. Decisions already made, with spike evidence (2026-10-18, on the laptop)

Spike code was throwaway (`/tmp/vwspike/pano3.py` on the laptop, with a viewer at `:8090`). The technique that worked (round 3):

- **Panorama:** cylindrical 360°, 2048 × 512, generated by **MultiDiffusion**. Overlapping 64 × 64-latent windows (512 px, SD-Turbo's native size), stride 32, wrapping around horizontally, with noise predictions averaged. Each window can have its own prompt.
- **Seam:** horizontal wrap-around, invisible in testing. The VAE encodes and decodes in 64-latent-wide tiles with an 8-latent wrap-around margin (a full-width VAE pass ran out of memory).
- **Region ahead:** windows within about ±30° of straight ahead get the base prompt plus "{discovery} ahead in the distance". The rest get the base prompt.
- **A step:**
  1. Rotate the panorama so the walking direction is at the centre.
  2. Dolly in by cropping the centre 1/1.15 and resizing back.
  3. Encode it, then run 4 denoising steps with **differential diffusion**. The region ahead (a feathered latent mask, full strength within ±0.08 of the width around centre and fading out over the next 0.06) is regenerated from pure noise. Everywhere else, the original noised latent is re-imposed until step `4 − int(4 × 0.6)`, so the background only changes like img2img at strength 0.6.
  4. Decode.
  5. Apply a 50% Lab colour transfer toward the first panorama of the current theme.
- **Prompt style:** "impressionist oil painting, visible brushstrokes, dreamlike, soft volumetric light". The mood is part of the base prompt (the round-3 haunted base was "a dark eerie haunted forest at night, a winding path, twisted dead trees, thick fog, cold moonlight").
- **Measured:** panorama 2.0 s, step 4.7 s, peak 3.26 GB allocated on the RTX 3050 Ti. Turning in the viewer ran at full frame rate.
- **Known artefacts:** occasional garbled "watermark" text, and a soft look because it's 512 px tall with 90° on screen, about 2.5× upscaled.
- **Model:** SD-Turbo for now. A quantized SDXL-Turbo (int8, with text encoders possibly offloaded to the CPU) is being compared for sharpness and variety. Whether to switch is decided on that measurement (§9).

## 6. How it works (to keep or carry over)

- **Viewer:** canvas-drawn 90° horizontal field of view into the cylindrical panorama (as in the spike viewer), with drag, A/D and arrows to turn. The walk transition is a dolly-in plus crossfade over about 0.9 s.
- **Facing direction** is a continuous yaw. W walks in the current yaw. The server rotates the panorama so that yaw is at the centre before generating.
- **Pre-generation:** while the player looks around, the server pre-generates the step for the direction they're facing, once the view has settled (my call: about 0.5 s without turning). If they turn well away (my call: more than 45°), the pre-generated spot is thrown out or replaced. Pressing W uses the ready spot if it matches their facing within that tolerance; otherwise it generates on demand and shows the holding animation.
- **GPU sharing:** one generation at a time on the GPU, queued fairly across players. On-demand steps take priority over pre-generation. Each player has at most one pre-generation in flight.
- **Discoveries:** a small instruction LLM on the CPU (my call: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct class) turns theme plus mood into a short list of discoveries ("an old lantern on a post", "a ruined stone chapel"). The list is refilled as it runs out, and it never shows text from the LLM other than the short discovery phrases. Each step's region ahead uses the next discovery. Some steps (my call: about 1 in 3) have no discovery, so the world has quiet stretches.
- **Discovery labels:** after a step, the object finder (CLIPSeg, already sped up about 5× in build 2) checks the region ahead for that step's discovery. A label appears only if it is really found (build 2's located-only rule and its threshold of 0.3). The label is tied to a yaw and height within the panorama, so it stays on the object as you turn. Clicking it turns you to face it and walks.
- **Theme drift:** a new theme fades in over the next 3 steps by blending old and new prompts. My call: interpolate the text-encoder embeddings 1/3, 2/3, 1. The colour reference resets once the new theme fully takes over.
- **Mood chips** add their words to the base prompt from the next step. The first spot after a chip change isn't pre-generated, or the pre-generated one is discarded.
- **Carried over from builds 1–2:** FastAPI app structure, per-player sessions with busy guard, TTL and LRU eviction, the error format and the 503-on-out-of-memory behaviour (with the UI keeping the previous view on failure), the colour-transfer helper, the always-visible label styling, the GPU, browser and unit test markers, and the run and deploy notes.
- **Retired:** the vocabulary, Qwen embedder, meaning-space index, mind-map, neighbour hotspots, and the zoom-crop steps.

### 6.1 Settled after review

- **What you walk toward survives (my call).** A step only regenerates the region ahead from noise when there's no located discovery there. If a discovery label is visible ahead, or you clicked one, that region is img2img'd (strength 0.6) from the dolly-in instead, so the object stays and comes closer. The next discovery is then placed beside it or beyond it: the windows just off-centre, or a farther band up toward the horizon. Its label carries over if CLIPSeg still finds it.
- **S = turn 180° and take a normal step (already decided with the user).** You end up facing the old "behind". It's the same pipeline as W.
- **Mood chips also move the colour reference (my call).** After a chip change, the first new spot becomes the colour reference, as with a theme change, so `night` or `snow` actually show.
- **Phones (my call):** on-screen ↑ walk and ↓ back buttons, plus drag to look. Phones are a bonus, not a test target beyond one mobile-viewport browser test.
- **Model comparison:** I run the SD-Turbo vs quantized SDXL-Turbo comparison before planning, and its result goes into §5. The generator sits behind an interface either way.
- **Retired code (my call):** the vocabulary, embedder, meaning-space index, mind-map and neighbour hotspots are removed from the Dream Walk branch along with their tests. They stay in git history on `slice1-playable` and `slice2-walking`.

## 7. Constraints

- RTX 3050 Ti laptop GPU, 4 GB (about 3.7 GB usable). The laptop has about 14 GB RAM, Ubuntu, kernel 7.0.0-38, driver 595.99.02. Development and running both happen on the laptop (`ssh gpu`, repo `~/dev/vecworld`).
- Everything local, with no paid APIs. The server runs on the host, not in Docker. The game server holds the GPU, so GPU tests need it stopped.
- Commits carry no Claude attribution.
- Latency targets: first panorama ≤ 5 s. A step that was pre-generated starts its transition in under 0.5 s, and one generated on demand finishes in about 6 s or less. **These are single-player targets (the user decided).** When several players move at once, they wait in the fair queue, so up to about 20 s with all 5 moving is acceptable. No batched multi-player generation and no admission limits in this build.

## 8. Left to the builder

The module layout inside the repo, exact window and batch sizes, the queue implementation, the LLM prompt for discoveries and its caching, CLIPSeg crop handling for the region ahead, label placement maths, the chip list beyond the six named, the negative prompt or crop for the watermark text, the upscaling approach (if any), and test strategy, following the build 1–2 conventions.

## 9. Open (decided by measurement, not by the user)

- **SD-Turbo or quantized SDXL-Turbo:** pick SDXL-Turbo only if it stays under about 3.6 GB peak and a step takes under about 8 s, *and* it's visibly sharper or more varied in a side-by-side walk. Otherwise stay on SD-Turbo.
- **Taller panorama (768 px) or an upscale pass:** only if it fits the same memory and latency limits.
